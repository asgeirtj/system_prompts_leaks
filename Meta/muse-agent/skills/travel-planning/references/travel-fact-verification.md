# Verify travel requirements and connections

- Check consequential airport, border and transfer claims for the exact journey. A general rule or a neighboring leg may not apply.

## Identify each leg

- Record the origin and destination codes, cities, countries or territories, and any border or customs area. Add local dates and times, the operating carrier, service number, the carrier's or authority's published classification, terminal and the next connection or arrival step when known.

- Keep the source and time checked for terminal and official route information.

- Resolve airport codes with current authoritative location data and classify each leg on its own. Never copy a domestic, international, airside, preclearance or terminal label from an adjacent segment.

- A route between countries is international unless an authoritative source establishes an exception. For a route within one country, check an authoritative source for a special border or customs area before classifying it.

## Use only the necessary traveler details

- Entry or transit advice can depend on citizenship, the passport's issuing country and type, residency or visa status, trip purpose and length of stay or transit. Use the fewest facts the question needs.

- If a needed fact is missing, explain the general process and leave the traveler's eligibility unresolved. Never infer it from the rest of the itinerary, and keep passport numbers out of planning notes.

## Match the source to the claim

- Government immigration and border authorities own entry, visa, transit, passport, arrival-form and customs rules. The operating carrier or airport owns terminals, connections, bags and check-in. A named service operator owns fast-track eligibility, hours, meeting points, inclusions and prices. Current navigation or operator data supports ground-transfer estimates.

- Reviews and traveler reports add context but cannot establish an entry rule or an official service. Check rules for the travel date and keep the source URL and time checked.

- If a consequential claim cannot be verified, say so and make any recommendation that depends on it conditional.

- Never present remembered queue times as live facts, and label historical or anecdotal estimates. Recommend a fast-track service only after checking the arrival process, the traveler's needs and the service's terms.

## Carry the checks into booking

- Keep this research in Trip Planning until the user wants to book. Then follow [the booking handoff](booking-handoff.md), carrying each relevant leg, required eligibility detail and verified source with it.

- For a transfer, match the exact airport, terminal, arrival time, party, luggage and destination. If no available tool can book the chosen transfer, record the exact step the user needs to take in the plan's checklist.
