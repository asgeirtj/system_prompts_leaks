# Finance presentations

Use for financial reporting, planning, valuation and investment decisions. Other decks do not need this guidance just because they contain numbers. Follow the user's terminology and template.

- State the result, outlook or decision and the evidence supporting it. Make clear which figures are actual results, forecasts or assumptions, and which statements are recommendations. Do not describe a prepared proposal as approved, or an approved plan as completed.
- When making a point with numbers, include the period, what is being compared and the amount needed to understand it. Explain a cause only when the evidence supports it; a target alone does not explain how it will be reached. A factual result does not need an added slogan or recommendation.
- Name the actual result, constraint, risk or action. Preserve precise technical terms and quotations. There is no universal banned-word list.
- Show currency, scale, percentages and multiples correctly. Do not attach currency to counts, years or ratios, or switch between millions and billions just for appearance. Keep positive and negative signs correct. An increase is not necessarily good news.
- Check that totals and numbers in the text agree with the same calculations. Keep assumptions and uncertainty on the slide when they affect a decision; put supporting detail and citations in notes where appropriate.
- Keep subtitles and short annotations only when they add information. Do not describe the process of making the deck, repeat the headline or invent a conclusion to fill space.

For chart labels, workbooks and arithmetic checks, use [editable tables and charts](../references/native_evidence.md). For layout, use [visual design guidance](../style_guidelines.md); for writing, follow [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md#writing).
